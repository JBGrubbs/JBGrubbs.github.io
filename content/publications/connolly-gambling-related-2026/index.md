abstract: 'Gambling-related knowledge is often considered central to gambling behavior, particularly in forms of gambling perceived as skill-based. However, gambling knowledge has been inconsistently defined and measured across studies. This systematic review examined how gambling knowledge has been conceptualized and assessed, as well as its associations with gambling outcomes. Following PRISMA guidelines, studies were identified through searches of PsycINFO, PubMed, and Web of Science. No age restrictions were applied; included studies examined samples ranging from children and adolescents to adults, although the majority drew on secondary school and university student samples. Findings indicated substantial heterogeneity in both definitions and measures of gambling knowledge, with no consensus conceptualization or standardized assessment approach. Associations between gambling knowledge and gambling outcomes varied depending on the operationalization used, with some forms of knowledge appearing protective and others associated with increased gambling risk. Overall, inconsistent conceptualization and measurement limit conclusions regarding the role of gambling knowledge in gambling behavior. Developing clearer and more objective measures may improve future research, screening, prevention, and intervention efforts.'
