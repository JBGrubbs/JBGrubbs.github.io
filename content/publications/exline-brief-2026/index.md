abstract: 'Our aim was to provide initial validation for the six-item Religious and Spiritual Struggles Scale (RSS-6), which uses broad items to capture the six domains of the Religious and Spiritual Struggles Scale (Exline, Pargament, Grubbs, & Yali, 2014). We also introduced the Religious and Spiritual Struggles Scale–14–Revised (RSS-14-R), a minor revision of the Religious and Spiritual Struggles Scale–14 (RSS-14; Exline et al., 2023) that replaces an item on lack of meaning with one on questioning whether one’s life will make a difference in the world. We drew data from a nationally representative, longitudinal sample (N = 3,964) recruited through YouGov. Participants completed the RSS-14-R at baseline and the RSS-6 1 week later. They completed distress measures (depression, anxiety, loneliness) at baseline, 6 months, and 1 year. The RSS-6 related in expected ways to the RSS-14-R. On the RSS-6, 62% of participants endorsed some struggle (at least “a little bit”) in the past few weeks (ultimate meaning: 42%; moral: 41%; interpersonal: 31%; doubt: 29%; divine: 25%; demonic: 21%). Both the RSS-6 and RSS-14-R correlated positively with emotional distress and predicted sustained distress over time. Participants who were younger, reported some religious afﬁliation, and did not identify as White, cisgender, or heterosexual reported more spiritual struggle. These results provide initial validation for the RSS-6 and RSS-14-R. They echo prior work showing that spiritual struggles are widespread and linked with mental health, cross-sectionally and over time. The RSS-6 may work especially well as a screener, as its broad categories can capture spiritual struggles not mentioned in other abbreviated Religious and Spiritual Struggles Scale versions.'
